What short-term rental management in Marrakech means

A property manager handles letting your home to guests: listings, pricing, guest welcome, housekeeping, linen, maintenance and payments. Marrakech is Morocco's leading tourist destination, with demand all year round and peaks in winter, spring and over the holidays.

Dar Serenity manages luxury apartments (Hivernage, Guéliz, Agdal, Ourika Road), villas with pools (Palmeraie, Amelkis, Amizmiz Road) and riads in the medina. Every property has one manager who knows it, visits it and answers you.

What a property earns on short-term lets in Marrakech

It depends on the type of property, the area, the finish and the pool. A well-located two-bedroom apartment lets at around MAD 900 a night; a villa with a pool between MAD 3,000 and 8,000 depending on the season. How it works

1. Visit and estimate. We visit your property, assess its potential and send you a figure.

2. Mandate and preparation. A two-page mandate. Professional photos, inventory, missing equipment on quotation.

3. Letting. Listings, pricing, guest welcome, hotel-standard housekeeping and maintenance.

4. You are paid. Detailed statement and transfer on the 5th of every month, with photos.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

What commission does a property manager charge in Marrakech?

Dar Serenity charges from 18% ex. VAT of collected income. No set-up fee, no minimum term: if the property does not let, you pay nothing.

Can I use my property whenever I want?

Yes. You block your dates in the calendar; upkeep, pool and garden continue during your stays.

Who deals with neighbours and the building management?

We do. Building rules are respected, guests are vetted and a manager reachable 7 days a week handles any incident.

Is a permit needed for short-term lets in Marrakech?

Furnished tourist accommodation must be declared and guest registration forms sent to the authorities. We handle these formalities for you.

How am I paid?

By bank transfer on the 5th of every month, in Morocco or abroad, with a detailed statement and an annual summary for your accountant.

Who holds the keys to my property?

We keep one set of keys at the office, handed over against an inventory and returned at the end of the mandate. Guests never have access to your personal keys or locked spaces.

What happens if something is damaged?

Photo report when the guest leaves, deduction from the security deposit, then a claim with the platform's insurance. For direct bookings, a deposit is collected before arrival. Your home insurance remains necessary for the building.

Who pays running costs and cleaning?

Water, electricity, internet, pool and garden remain at your expense and appear on the statement. Cleaning and linen are charged to guests as stay fees. Small repairs are included in our commission.

Who declares the rental income?

You do. Rental income is taxable in Morocco; we send you a yearly summary for your accountant and can refer you to a partner accountant. We do not give tax advice.
